Noon position: step by step

  1. About 0900, morning sight (3/O). Take the Sun with the sextant (lower limb). Write down the ship's time to the second and the sextant altitude.
  2. Ship and DR (left column). Enter the position at that time (GPS or DR), course and speed, ship's date, UTC offset, height of eye and index error.
  3. Sights, row 1. Type the time and sextant altitude of the morning sight. The Fix card shows the morning position line at once.
  4. Read the meridian passage line above the sights: the time the Sun crosses your meridian (for the moving ship), its bearing, the sextant altitude to preset and the time to be ready. Row 2 time is filled in by itself. No almanac is needed.
  5. At noon (2/M). Be on the bridge by the "be ready by" time. Set the sextant to the altitude shown and keep the Sun on the horizon as it rises. When it stops rising and starts to dip, read the highest altitude.
  6. Sights, row 2. Type that highest altitude. Leave the time as filled in.
  7. Fix card. The noon position at 12:00 ship's time is shown. Type the GPS position at noon (Ship and DR, optional) to see how far the fix is from the GPS. Press Print sheet for the record, for the master to check.

If the course or speed changes after the morning sight, change them before noon: the meridian passage time and the run to noon use them. If the clocks are changed, change the UTC offset.
