How it is worked, and how accurate it is
  1. The Sun's position is computed for each moment (about 0.003° accuracy) and the moment its altitude reaches the target is found to the second, at your own position. The result is rounded to the nearest minute.
  2. Sunrise and sunset are when the Sun's upper limb is on the horizon: centre at −(34′ refraction + 16′ semi-diameter), about −0°50′. This is the Nautical Almanac rule.
  3. Twilight: civil begins and ends with the Sun's centre at −6°, nautical at −12° (the time for star sights, when the horizon and the stars are both visible), astronomical at −18°.
  4. Height of eye lowers the horizon by the dip, 1.76 × √height (arc minutes), so the Sun appears earlier and disappears later.
  5. Ship's time = UTC + zone. LMT = UTC + longitude ÷ 15.
  6. Checked against a full planetary ephemeris at 420+ events from 60°S to 66°N: the largest difference was 11 seconds. Real refraction depends on temperature and pressure, so allow about a minute for the weather, more near the poles.
  7. Near the poles the Sun may not rise or set, or twilight may not end: the table says so instead of showing a time.
